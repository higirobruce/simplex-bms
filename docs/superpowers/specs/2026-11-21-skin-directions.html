Outcome

Cloud & Azure was chosen; my pick was Graphite & Lime

Kept as written, so the reasoning that lost is still legible. The tokens now live in src/app/globals.css and the contrast contracts are asserted in tests/skin/tokens.test.ts.

  • It keeps a thread to what exists. Shelf Edge already uses hi-vis yellow #FFD400. Acid lime is that idea grown up, so the back office we already shipped doesn't become a stranger to the lane.
  • One saturated colour is functionally right here. On a till, exactly one thing must be findable without looking — the total. Give it the only chromatic colour on screen and the cashier's eye has nowhere else to go.
  • It isn't the default. Two of your three references are blue, and blue POS dashboards are everywhere. Azure is the safe one; it is also the one nobody remembers.
  • Bone & Forest is the genuinely quiet option — the most minimal of the three and the most grown-up. If "minimal" matters more to you than "modern", pick that one; it is the only direction here that would still look right in five years.
The rule: lime touches one element per screen.

Used twice it stops meaning anything, and an acid colour used loosely reads as a toy — which is fatal for something that prints tax receipts. Total tile, active line marker, primary key. Never all three at once.

Cost check

What this does to the egui decision

I argued yesterday that the port was cheap because Shelf Edge was flat — 2px corners, no shadows. This skin is softer, so I owe you the revised number rather than letting that argument quietly expire.

  • Rounding is free. egui draws rounded rectangles natively; a 10px corner costs the same as a 2px one.
  • Hairlines are free. 1px strokes are what it is best at, and this grammar leans on borders rather than heavy rules.
  • Soft shadows are the weak spot. egui's shadow support is limited, so I have built these panels with hairlines and a tinted second surface instead — which is more minimal anyway, and closer to your first reference than a drop-shadow would be.
  • Fonts embed fine. All four families here are TTFs that go straight into the binary.

Net: the port stays cheap. The one thing I would not sign up for is a shadow-heavy, layered-glass skin — that is where a native UI starts fighting you.

Pick a direction and I will rewrite the tokens in src/app/globals.css, update tests/skin/tokens.test.ts, and restate the skin section of the design doc. The back office and the lane move together — that is the whole point of keeping one token set.
