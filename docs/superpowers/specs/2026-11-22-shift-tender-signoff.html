01 — Why this slice existed

Every sale the till recorded was unsyncable

The cloud's Sale.shiftId is non-null and a Shift model already existed there. The till had no shift concept at all and sent no shiftId — so nothing it recorded could be stored in the back office.

That reframed the work. Shift tracking was not a reporting feature to add after mixed tender; it was a prerequisite for anything syncing at all. Then a review found the gap one layer down: the cloud also needs laneId, and the till had never sent a shift to the cloud either — so a queued sale would still fail on a foreign key to a row the back office had never seen. That became Task 9, added mid-execution.

02 — Three times I was wrong

Corrections, in the order they happened

"The customer is overcharged"

I justified adding a whole task on the claim that a card overpayment settles and silently overcharges. It does not — settle returns OverTenderOnNonCash. The implementer caught it by reading the code instead of my plan. The task stood on a smaller claim: an operational dead-end, not a money loss.

"A reorder would misreport the payment method"

I marked this must-fix. The final reviewer overturned it: header_code seeds from the first entry then scans all codes, breaking ties on the lower wire code — provably order-independent. I verified that myself before accepting the reversal.

"These are not fiscal documents"

The costliest. I told the implementer the X and Z reports were internal and never pointed them at CIS §18/§19 — which are mandatory content lists, and define a Z as a daily business and accounting record. Ours was per shift and headed "Z REPORT · FINAL".

The pattern in all three

Each came from reasoning off a summary — a previous review's wording, my own plan text — instead of the source. The same failure produced the reprint defect in the previous slice, where three reviews confirmed a bug because all three checked the code against my brief rather than the specification.

03 — What the reviews caught that tests could not

Four findings worth the seats they cost

  • A barcode could open a shift. A scanner types digits then Enter — exactly how the opening float is keyed. Waving any product at an idle till would have opened a shift with a 13-digit float. No test or spec line would have surfaced it; it comes from picturing the counter.
  • A race that manufactures an accusation. Shift totals made two un-snapshotted reads. A sale confirming between them had its cash counted but its change never subtracted, overstating expected cash — so a supervisor reading an X report mid-trading would count the drawer and conclude it was short.
  • A successful close showed the cashier nothing. The figures went to Notice::Info; the screen rendered only Notice::Problem. Feedback exactly inverted — you saw the numbers only when printing failed. The test read state instead of the painted frame, so it passed.
  • A hardcoded shift time. I had deferred "Shift 3 · open 06:58" as cosmetic. The tell: opened_at had no non-test reader anywhere — the real value was available all along with a mock in its seat.

05 — Shipping with these known gaps

Written into the code, not just this page

  • The CIS §18/§19 daily X and Z reports are not implemented. They need aggregation across shifts for a trading day and a per-tax-rate split that ShiftTotals does not gather. Documented in report.rs.
  • Nothing drains the outbound queue yet. The cloud handler for kind='shift' is Phase 3.
  • No backfill for shifts opened before this change — moot, since no till has traded.
  • A recomposed close report is always marked COPY, because after a restart nothing records whether the original reached paper.
  • Refunds, suspend/resume, discounts and buyer TIN remain later slices, as does the supervisor gate on cancelling a pending sale.
